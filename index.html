Speech AI · in production

Two flows, two engines, one reliable service

  • Voicemail, accuracy-first: NVIDIA Canary, with automatic fallback to Whisper when output is degenerate, and a final failover to the shared vLLM tier.
  • Call recordings, throughput-first: Whisper served on vLLM behind a load balancer, Redis priority queues (0–99) with live re-prioritisation, and Celery workers on stateless HA nodes.
  • Long audio: 45-minute chunks up to 10 hours, with timestamps kept absolute across chunks; automatic language ID.
  • Speaker diarization: Silero VAD per channel for stereo; ECAPA-TDNN embeddings with agglomerative clustering for mono.
  • LLM analytics that never block transcription: Gemma summaries and keywords on a separate queue and load balancer, delivered by webhook or polling.
  • Operations: hash-based duplicate suppression, per-tenant correction rules, CUDA-error watchdog, fork-safe PyTorch in Celery.

Lesson I carry everywhere: a 200 OK isn't success. Validate the model's output, not the status code, and fail over automatically.

How I build AI products

Three principles from shipping systems that real people depend on.

01

Treat model output as untrusted

Structured outputs, validation and guardrails in code, and a human in the loop for high-risk actions. Rules decide; the model reads and writes.

02

Design for cost and failure

Return fast, queue behind, put model tiers behind load balancers with failover, and route to small models first, escalating only when needed.

03

Measure everything

Every change ships with an eval set and a CI regression gate. I track recall, latency and cost per request, so quality is provable, not anecdotal.
